# Meaning by Design: A Practical Mini Textbook

This guide explores how communication gives ordinary products, organizations, and experiences recognizable meaning. It connects persuasion, brand archetypes, visual design traditions, and AI-assisted working practices through one recurring example: the plain white T-shirt.

## Read the Chapters in Order

1. [Chapter 1: Persuasion and Human Decision-Making](01-persuasion.md) - how communication can shape attention, interpretation, trust, and action.
2. [Chapter 2: Brand Archetypes and Meaning](02-archetypes.md) - how products can invite audiences to feel, imagine, or become something.
3. [Chapter 3: Design Language, Modernism, and Postmodernism](03-design-language.md) - how visual traditions communicate through structure, typography, and style.
4. [Chapter 4: One White T-Shirt, Four Different Products](04-white-tshirt-case-study.md) - a case study combining persuasion, archetype, and design language.
5. [Chapter 5: From Creative Intent to Reliable Work](05-synthesis.md) - how the three lenses can direct bounded AI-assisted work with checks and human review.

## How to Use This Guide

Read the first three chapters as a set of lenses. Then use Chapter 4 to compare several presentations of the same physical product. Finish with Chapter 5 to turn the ideas into a practical workflow for planning, directing, checking, and reviewing AI-assisted work.

As you read, separate three things: what a design visibly does, what meaning it appears to invite, and what evidence would be needed to support a factual claim. Pause at the questions and try them on a product, website, poster, or assignment of your own. The goal is not to memorize labels; it is to make creative decisions more intentional and easier to review.
